Why Buzzkill exists

Your focus didn't break. It got taken.

We now switch screens about every 47 seconds. A generation is growing up with a phone that's built to pull them away. Buzzkill is our attempt to win some of that attention back, together.

The trend

We stay on one screen for less than a minute

For two decades, Gloria Mark's team at UC Irvine has timed how long people stay on one screen before switching to something else. The number keeps falling.

Myth check

No, you don't have the attention span of a goldfish

The famous "8 seconds, shorter than a goldfish" stat came from a 2015 marketing report that cited a website no one could trace back to real research. Attention researchers say our basic ability to focus hasn't measurably changed.

What changed is everything around us: more pings, more tabs, and a phone within reach all day. So the fix isn't a better brain. It's a better environment.

The evidence

Students have a phone problem. We built the fix.

Three studies, three ways phones hurt studying, and the part of Buzzkill that answers each one. Every card links to the paper.

Phones eat a quarter of class time

Read the paper →

What they found

  • Researchers tracked the phones of 84 college students in class for 14 weeks.
  • Students were on their phones for over 25% of class time.
  • A phone distraction happened every 3–4 minutes and lasted over a minute.
  • More phone use in class went with lower grades.

Kim, Kim, Kim, Kim, Han, Lee, Mark & Lee, "Understanding smartphone usage in college classrooms: A long-term measurement study," Computers & Education, 2019.

How Buzzkill fixes it

Every pickup gets caught and counted

The 3–4 minute check-in habit only works because nobody notices. Buzzkill notices every single pickup, roasts you out loud, and makes you shake it off.

Your pickups per hour and your longest focus stretch show up on your dashboard and the leaderboard, so you can watch the habit shrink.

Even a buzz you ignore breaks your focus

Read the paper →

What they found

  • People did an attention task while some got calls or texts on their own phone.
  • They never touched the phone, and still made more mistakes.
  • The drop was about as big as the one from actually using a phone.
  • Why: a notification starts your mind wandering, and that lasts after the buzz ends.

Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert, "The Attentional Cost of Receiving a Cell Phone Notification," Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 2015.

How Buzzkill fixes it

Face down, and checking waits for the break

Phones lie face down for the whole session, so a lit-up screen can't pull you in. And since picking up costs you, there's no point in "just checking."

The group votes on a pomodoro (like 25 minutes of focus, then 5 of break). Breaks are free phone time, so you know exactly when you can look.

Phone distraction makes you forget what you just learned

Read the paper →

What they found

  • A meta-analysis combining 27 randomized experiments with 2,245 participants.
  • Phone distraction had a medium-sized negative effect on what students remembered right after learning.
  • The hit was biggest for lectures. (Results for reading were mixed, so the authors call that part tentative.)

Chen, Yan, Moeyaert & Bangert-Drowns, "Mobile Multitasking in Learning: A Meta-Analysis of Effects of Mobilephone Distraction on Young Adults' Immediate Recall," SSRN preprint, 2024.

Winning it back

What the research says helps, and how Buzzkill uses it

Research

Distance beats willpower

Just having your phone nearby uses up focus, because part of your brain is busy not checking it. Out of reach did best.

Phones go face down, and lifting one costs you

Research

Phone-free rooms help struggling students most

In a randomized trial at 10 colleges in India, phone-free lectures raised grades slightly (0.07 standard deviations), with the biggest gains for students who were behind.

Interruptions are expensive

Each switch can cost over 20 minutes of getting back on track, so long, protected blocks of work matter.
